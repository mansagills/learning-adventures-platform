# Submitting a game to Learning Adventures

Thank you for making a game! **Focus on the learning.** You don't need to
worry about art, sound, animation, saving or phone layouts. When your game
is added here, we rebuild it in the Learning Adventures style:

- pixel art with its own characters
- music
- levels that adapt to each child
- hints
- a page for parents and teachers

We keep your questions, facts and ideas.

## What to send

**One HTML file** that opens in a browser and plays. It can be plain and
simple: buttons and text are fine.

At the top of the file, add a comment like this (it helps us more than
anything else in the game):

```html
<!--
  Game: Fraction Bakery
  Author: <your name>
  Grades: 3–4
  Subject: math            (math, science, english, history or interdisciplinary)
  Standards: 3.NF.1, 3.NF.3

  What children should learn:
    - Fractions as equal parts of a whole
    - Comparing fractions with the same numerator or denominator

  Mistakes children often make (and what they think):
    - "1/4 is bigger than 1/3 because 4 is bigger than 3"
    - Parts that are not equal still count as fourths

  Ideas for harder levels:
    - Compare fractions with different denominators using a picture

  Facts and sources (science and history games):
    - "The Moon has no light of its own" (NASA, moon.nasa.gov)
-->
```

## Tips that make your game better

- **Write the wrong answers on purpose.** The best wrong answers come from
  a real mistake ("1/4 because 4 > 3"), not from random numbers. Say which
  mistake each one comes from.
- **Say why an answer is wrong.** One short sentence per mistake is perfect.
- **Give more than one level** if you can: easy, medium, hard.
- **Check your facts** with a reliable source (NASA, NOAA, USGS, National
  Park Service, Smithsonian, or a textbook) and list it.
- **Don't use outside links, trackers or sign-ins.** This is a site for
  children.

## How to add it

Save the file here as `games-src/submissions/<game-name>.html`, using
lowercase words joined by dashes, like `fraction-bakery.html`. Or send it
to Mansa, who adds it.

## What happens next

1. We study your game.
2. We write a plan for the new version (setting, characters, levels,
   mistakes it catches).
3. Mansa approves the plan.
4. We build and test the new version.
5. It goes on the site, with you credited on its "For grown-ups" page.

Your original file stays in this folder as the record of your work.
