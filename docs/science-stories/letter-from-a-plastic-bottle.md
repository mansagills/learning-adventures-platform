# Letter from a Plastic Bottle

*Creative writing, topic 6 of the Chemistry Story Ideas. This is the story seed for a future science game.*

---

Dear Friend,

I know you don't usually get mail from a bottle. But I have something to tell you, and I only have a little time left to say it.

My name is Bo. My full name is Polyethylene Terephthalate, but that is a lot to say, so my friends call me PET. I am a plastic bottle, and this is the story of my life.

## Where I Began

I was born a very long time ago, long before I was ever shaped like a bottle.

Millions of years ago, tiny sea plants and animals lived, died and settled deep underground. Slowly, heat and pressure turned them into oil. That oil was my great-great-grandparent.

Then people drilled it up and sent it to a factory. There, scientists did something clever. They took tiny pieces of oil called **molecules** and hooked them together, one by one, like beads on a string. A long chain of molecules is called a **polymer**. That's what I'm made of. Billions of molecules, holding hands.

Then I was warmed up and blown into the shape of a bottle, like blowing a balloon inside a mold. I came out clear, shiny and light as a feather.

## My Short, Busy Life

I was filled with cold, clean water. Then I rode in a truck, sat on a shelf and went home in a shopping bag.

A girl named Maya opened my cap. *Pssst!* She drank me up during a soccer game. It took about ten minutes.

Ten minutes! I took millions of years to make and about ten minutes to use. That's the part that makes me a little sad.

## The Part I Wish I Could Change

Maya was in a hurry, and I ended up on the ground by the park fence.

Here is a secret about plastic. **I do not rot like an apple core.** Tiny living things, called microbes, are the world's great clean-up crew. They eat leaves, fruit and wood. But my chain of molecules is so strong that they barely know what to do with me.

Rain washed me into a storm drain. The drain led to a creek, and the creek led to a river, and the river carried me to the sea.

Out there, I bobbed along for a long, long time. The sun and the waves slowly broke me into smaller and smaller pieces. These tiny bits are called **microplastics**. Some are smaller than a grain of sand. Fish and seabirds sometimes mistake them for food, and that isn't good for them.

I was not trying to hurt anyone. I was just a bottle. But I learned that things don't disappear when we throw them "away." There is no "away." There is only somewhere else.

## But Here Is the Good News

I was one of the lucky ones. A beach clean-up crew found me, and a boy named Omar fished me out of the sand. He dropped me in a **recycling bin**. I almost cried, and I don't even have eyes.

At the recycling center I was sorted, squished, washed and shredded into tiny flakes. The flakes were melted and spun into new things. I might become:

- a warm fleece jacket 🧥
- a pair of sneakers
- a carpet for a classroom
- or even... **another bottle!**

That's the amazing thing about chemistry. My molecules aren't gone. They can be taken apart and put back together, again and again. I don't have to be trash. I can be a *resource*.

## What I Hope You Will Do

You don't have to be a grown-up scientist to help me and my family. Here is my wish list:

1. **Refuse** what you don't need. Do you need a new bottle, or can you use one you already have?
2. **Reuse** a water bottle again and again.
3. **Recycle** the right way. Empty me, rinse me and put me in the right bin.
4. **Remember** that small choices add up. One bottle is small, but a whole school of kids is a mighty team.
5. **Invent!** The world needs young chemists to dream up plastics that break down safely, and better ways to recycle. Maybe that's you.

## One Last Thing

Right now, I am floating in a bin, waiting for my next life. I don't know yet what I will become. Maybe I'll be a bottle again. Maybe I'll be a jacket on a cold day, or a seat in a school bus.

Whatever I become, I hope I'm used well.

Thank you for reading my letter. Thank you for caring about the planet. And the next time you finish a drink, remember me.

With a hopeful heart,

**Bo the Plastic Bottle**
*(Polyethylene Terephthalate, but you can call me PET)*

P.S. Don't forget to put my cap back on before you recycle me.

## Science Ideas Hidden in the Story (for the game)

| Idea in the story | Science concept |
| --- | --- |
| Oil turned into plastic | Fossil fuels, raw materials |
| "Beads on a string" | Monomers and polymers |
| Melting and reshaping flakes | Physical change, and recycling as a change that can be reversed |
| Plastic doesn't rot | Biodegradable vs. non-biodegradable |
| Tiny pieces in the ocean | Microplastics, food chains, ocean pollution |
| Reduce, Reuse, Recycle | Conservation and the 3 Rs |
| "Invent a better plastic" | Green chemistry, and careers in science |
