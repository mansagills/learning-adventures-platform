# Stage 7 — Post-launch

**Question:** How do you keep the game alive and the players around?

The game is out. There is still work to do, and the first two weeks set the trajectory for
everything after.

---

## The Four Workstreams

### 1. Bug fixes
Anything you didn't have time to kill before launch, plus everything players discover that you
never would have. Patch them out and tidy the loose ends. Prioritise by how many players hit it
× how badly it hurts — not by how interesting the bug is.

Set a cadence early: hotfixes for progression blockers within days, everything else on a regular
patch rhythm players can rely on.

### 2. DLC
Paid content is the natural home for things cut to meet deadlines — the scope ledger from stages
2 and 3 is your DLC backlog, already designed. It also keeps the game visible and relevant long
after release.

Rule: DLC extends the game, it doesn't complete it. Content that players expected in the base
game will be read as having been cut *for* the DLC, and the reputational cost outweighs the
revenue.

### 3. Free content updates
The one thing players like more than paid DLC is free DLC. Free updates drive returning players,
sales spikes, storefront visibility, and goodwill that paid content cannot buy. Plan a mix: free
updates carry the relationship, paid DLC carries the revenue.

### 4. Balance patches
Post-launch play surfaces dominant strategies no internal test found — Modern Warfare 2's akimbo
Model 1887s were miserable for months, and the nerf was genuinely celebrated. Watch usage and
win-rate data, not just forum volume, then act.

Balance discipline: change one variable at a time, publish the reasoning, give changes time to
settle before the next pass, and never balance solely on the loudest complaint.

---

## Watch These

| Signal | Reading |
|--------|---------|
| Refund rate | Store page or price promised something the game isn't |
| Week-2 retention cliff | Content runs out, or the loop doesn't sustain |
| Common bug reports | Test coverage gaps — feed back into the test plan |
| Review themes | Split fixable (bugs, QoL, balance) from unfixable (genre mismatch) |
| Where drop-off clusters | The difficulty or pacing wall to patch next |

---

## Closing The Loop

Run a postmortem while it's fresh: what the estimates missed, which stage got skipped and what it
cost, which cuts were right, which were wrong. That document is the most valuable asset you carry
into the next game's planning stage.

---

## Deliverables

- `docs/game/post-launch-plan.md` — patch cadence, DLC roadmap, free-update plan, balance process
- Live bug triage pipeline
- Analytics dashboard covering retention, progression, drop-off
- Postmortem doc

---

## Exit Criteria

There isn't one — post-launch ends when you decide it ends. Make that an explicit decision:
announce the final update, say so publicly, and leave the game in a state that stands on its own.
Silent abandonment is what players remember.

- [ ] Patch cadence published and met
- [ ] Launch-window bugs resolved or scheduled
- [ ] DLC / free-update roadmap decided and communicated
- [ ] Balance process running on data
- [ ] Postmortem written and fed into the next project's stage 1
