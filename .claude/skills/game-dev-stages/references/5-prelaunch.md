# Stage 5 — Pre-launch

**Question:** Will anyone know it exists?

Marketing kicks into gear. You should have a stable beta build ready to show the public — this
is the first stage where people outside the team form an opinion, and opinions are sticky.

---

## The Work

### The beta build

Public-facing builds get their own bar: no crashes in the first fifteen minutes, no
progression blockers on the demo path, no placeholder assets in anything visible, and a clean
first-run experience on a machine that has never seen the game.

### Choose your reveal shape

| Vehicle | Good for | Cost |
|---------|----------|------|
| **Trailer** | Reach, wishlists, press pickup | Capture, edit, music licensing |
| **Playable demo** | Conversion, real feedback, festival eligibility | A separately maintained build |
| **Early Access** | Funding plus a live feedback loop | A permanent commitment to update publicly |
| **Beta / closed test** | Load and balance data without public exposure | Testers, feedback processing |

### Time it against the calendar

Studios routinely tie reveals to events and showcases — E3-style trade shows, PAX, Nintendo
Directs, Sony State of Play, Steam Next Fest, itch.io events. A slot in someone else's audience
is worth more than a launch into silence. Check submission deadlines; they land months before
the event.

### Build the storefront

Store page live with capsule art, screenshots, trailer, tags, and honest copy. Wishlists start
accumulating from this point and are the single best predictor of launch-day sales. Get the page
up early even if the date is "coming soon".

Also: press/creator list, review-key plan, community channel, mailing list, social presence,
press kit (logo, key art, screenshots, factsheet, contact).

---

## Handling The Reception

You will get feedback you didn't want. The model to follow is *Bloodstained: Ritual Of The
Night* — heavily criticised for weak visuals, the team responded with a trailer that opened on a
collage of the negative feedback before producer Koji Igarashi threw his wine glass down and
declared he'd prove them wrong. The visuals were overhauled, and the audience rewarded the
willingness to listen with strong reviews.

The transferable part is not the theatre. It's: **take the criticism seriously, fix the actual
thing, and show the fix.** Then check what the fix costs — if it's a re-plan, price it as one
rather than absorbing it into an already-set date.

Sort feedback into: *fix before launch* / *fix post-launch* / *working as intended, explain it* /
*not our audience*. All four are legitimate outcomes.

---

## Deliverables

- Stable public beta build
- Trailer and/or demo
- Store pages live on all launch platforms
- Press kit and outreach list
- `docs/game/launch-checklist.md` started
- Feedback log with triage decisions

---

## Exit Criteria

- [ ] Public build stable and representative of the final game
- [ ] Store pages live, wishlists accumulating
- [ ] Trailer published; demo shipped if planned
- [ ] Press/creator outreach sent, review keys ready
- [ ] Community channel active and monitored
- [ ] Launch date set and defensible against the remaining bug list
- [ ] Platform certification submitted where required, with lead times understood
- [ ] Public feedback triaged; anything marked "fix before launch" is scheduled
